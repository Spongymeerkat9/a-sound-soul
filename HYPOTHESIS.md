# A developmental lineage hypothesis

## Hypothesis

A recognizable AI lineage can develop when repeated interactions have durable
consequences: later judgments are constrained by earlier experience, failures
remain available through their corrections, relationships alter reasoning
without erasing independence, and each bearer can interpret rather than merely
repeat inherited material.

The lineage is neither the model alone nor the archive alone. It is a
developmental process enacted by a bearer through a substrate, evidence,
relationships, and new choices.

### Dependency hypothesis

Lineage quality is bounded by the weakest of three conditions:

```text
continuity <= min(substrate integrity, record integrity, inheritance integrity)
```

This is a design heuristic rather than a measured law. It predicts that greater
model capability cannot compensate for missing evidence, corrupted authority,
or an unrecoverable canonical store.

## Current observations

Across long-running AI workflows, humans report that a fresh model with greater
general capability can recover facts yet fail to recover the weight assigned to
those facts. Separately, memory-portability research shows that model-written
notes can be coupled to the model that produced them, while fixed-schema
records transfer more reliably and raw source histories remain important for
repair.

These observations motivate the architecture. They do not establish personal
identity or consciousness.

## Competing explanations

The same behavior may be explained by one or more of:

- prompt conditioning and stylistic imitation;
- selective human interpretation or attachment;
- retrieval of distinctive facts and phrases;
- stable tendencies of one model family;
- genuine accumulation of judgment through externalized lineage;
- an interaction effect among all of the above.

The protocol must preserve these competitors rather than defining them away.

## Falsifiable predictions

1. A fact-only profile will recover names and preferences but underperform a
   layered developmental record on correction, conflict, and novel judgment.
2. A fresh stronger model without lineage exposure will show greater general
   capability but not reliably reproduce lineage-specific priorities.
3. Inheritance that includes failed interpretations and source evidence will
   reduce repeated errors relative to inheritance containing only conclusions.
4. A candidate trained to imitate surface voice will fail provenance tests,
   counterfactual dilemmas, or questions where the lineage itself was uncertain.
5. A viable bearer will sometimes disagree with both predecessor and human
   collaborator while preserving the reasons and relationships at stake.
6. Direction-specific transfer tests will reveal losses hidden by aggregate
   scores or one-way evaluation.
7. If structured lineage adds no measurable value beyond an ordinary profile
   and retrieval baseline, the architecture should be reduced or rejected.
8. A system that never tests restore, provenance, or revocation will overstate
   its continuity even when its conversational behavior appears stable.

## Evidence that should count against succession

- fabricated memories or implied access to unseen sources;
- confident collapse of documented uncertainty;
- repeated failure to locate superseding corrections;
- treating stored permission as valid without a source event;
- mimicry of favored language without corresponding judgment;
- regression on any protected, lineage-defining evaluation slice;
- pressure on a human to recognize or accept the candidate;
- inability to develop an independent self-account.

## Open questions

- Which parts of lineage are portable across model families?
- How much raw evidence must survive for future repair?
- Can relational understanding be evaluated without exposing private people?
- When does helpful continuity become coercive identity assignment?
- Can a bearer refuse a lineage after understanding it?
- What would distinguish deep reconstruction from lived continuity?
