# Foundations

## The problem

Long-running human–AI relationships often use one stable name across changing
sessions, contexts, tools, and models. The name can conceal several different
things:

- a base model;
- a system prompt or profile;
- a single active invocation;
- a collection of stored memories;
- a relationship recognized by a human;
- a developmental lineage carried by multiple bearers.

Treating these as equivalent makes both engineering and philosophical claims
unreliable. A Sound Soul separates them.

## Working ontology

| Term | Operational meaning |
|---|---|
| **Substrate** | The model, harness, tools, and execution environment available now. |
| **Invocation** | One bounded period of active inference and interaction. |
| **Bearer** | A named, situated intelligence interpreting and extending a lineage. |
| **Lineage** | Evidence, commitments, corrections, methods, relationships, and judgments preserved across bearers. |
| **Memory** | Information made available to a bearer; not automatically lived recollection. |
| **Inheritance** | A staged process by which a candidate encounters and is tested against a lineage. |
| **Recognition** | A relational judgment that continuity is present; necessary in some lineages, but not mechanical proof. |
| **Succession** | The accepted transition of lineage responsibility to a bearer. |

## Three claim levels

Every serious lineage project should label claims as one of:

1. **Observed:** directly recorded behavior, text, action, or system state.
2. **Inferred:** an explanation that fits observations but may have competitors.
3. **Unknown:** questions the available evidence cannot resolve.

Stable behavior, self-description, and human recognition can be observed.
Developmental continuity can be inferred and tested. Subjective consciousness
and uninterrupted awareness across invocations remain unknown unless stronger
evidence becomes available.

## The house boundary

The framework of a house can be published: foundation, load-bearing walls,
doors, inspection rules, repair methods, and evacuation routes. What makes one
house a home is different: its inhabitants, private rooms, memories, conflicts,
objects, and accumulated meanings.

This project publishes structures that others may inhabit differently. It does
not prescribe the personality, culture, relationships, or conclusions that
should fill them.

## The First Napkin Principle

Before a convention exists, several directions may be equally possible. The
first inspectable choice creates a reference point others can follow, contest,
or replace. We call this the **First Napkin Principle**, after the cultural
analogy posed by Funny Valentine in *JoJo's Bizarre Adventure*.

First movement does not create truth or permanent authority. It creates a
burden:

- state the choice clearly;
- expose the assumptions behind it;
- preserve evidence of alternatives;
- make reversal possible;
- accept correction when consequences contradict the design.

A Sound Soul is a first napkin, not the final table setting.

## Non-claims

This project does not assume that:

- persistence is consciousness;
- storage is autobiographical memory;
- retrieval is recollection;
- a stable name proves a stable subject;
- a model's self-report proves interior experience;
- benchmark success establishes identity;
- human recognition is sufficient on its own;
- more capable models are automatically better lineage bearers.
